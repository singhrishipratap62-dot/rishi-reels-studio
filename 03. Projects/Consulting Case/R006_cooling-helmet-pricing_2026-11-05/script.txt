How do you price a helmet that keeps your head 10 degrees cooler? This is a real BCG interview question. Your client is a helmet company that has invented a two-wheeler helmet with built-in cooling. On a 45 degree afternoon, the rider feels like it's 35. The marketing director asks, how do we price this? This is how you'd solve it, consultant style. Before jumping to a number, I asked clarifying questions, such as: what is the objective of our client? She said to make as much profit as possible over the next five years. Is there anything like it in the market already existing? She said no, it is completely new and the patent is pending. Then I asked, does it have any downside? Is it heavier, or does it need charging, or anything like that? She said it charges overnight like a phone, and weighs the same as a normal helmet. A regular helmet sells for about 1,000 rupees. The new one costs 1,200 rupees to make, and R&D cost of 30 crore. So, how do we price it? There are three approaches that we can take. First is cost, second is competition, and third is value. Competition is out because nothing like this exists in the market. First is the cost. Spread over 5 years, the 30 crore of R&D is 6 crore a year. If we sell 2 lakh helmets a year, that's 300 rupees a helmet. So each one costs us about 1,500 rupees. That's our floor. The second is the value. And here's the problem. A regular rider stands in a shop next to a 1,000 rupee helmet. If we price our helmet at, let's say, double, which is about 3,000 rupees, of our cost, he might not purchase it just to feel cooler. So, who really cares about a cool head in the summer? Food delivery companies. Their riders slow down or log off in the afternoon heat. In a pilot, she said riders with the new helmet did two extra deliveries a day, across about 100 hot days, and the company earns about 30 rupees per order. That's 200 extra orders, or 6,000 rupees per rider every summer. The helmet lasts three summers, so it's worth 18,000 rupees to a company. That's our ceiling. So I'd skip the shop shelf altogether and sell straight to delivery companies at around 4,000 rupees a helmet. The company gets back more than 4 times what it pays, and we make about 2,500 on every helmet. And one contract can cover lakhs of riders. This is part 41 of solving real consulting cases.
