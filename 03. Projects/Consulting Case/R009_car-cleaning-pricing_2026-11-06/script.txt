How do you price a service that cleans your car every morning before you wake up? This is a real BCG interview question. Your client is a startup in Gurgaon that sends a trained cleaner to wash your car daily, with a full interior clean every week. The founder has asked you: what should we charge per month? This is how you would solve it, consultant style. Before jumping to a number, I would ask clarifying questions, such as: what is the objective of the client? She said it has to become profitable within the first or second year. Is there anything like it in the market? She said local cleaners charge about 600 rupees a month, but they skip days and only wipe from the outside. Then I asked: what does it cost us? She said a cleaner earns 15,000 rupees a month. If the cars are spread across different homes, a cleaner manages to do about 20 cars a day, but in one housing society he can do about 30. Supplies and app cost us about 200 rupees per car a month. Winning a customer through online ads costs about 2,000 rupees, but through a camp inside a society only about 500. A customer stays about 10 months. So how do we price it? There are three approaches that we can look at here. First, cost; second, competition; and third, value. First is the cost approach. For cars spread across homes, the cleaner costs 750 rupees per car, plus 200 for supplies, plus 200 a month to win a customer. That's 1,150 rupees. Inside one society, the cleaner cost drops to 500, and winning the customer costs just 50 rupees a month. That's about 750 rupees. Second is the competition. A local cleaner is about 600 rupees, so customers will compare us to that itself. And third is value. A weekly interior clean at a car wash costs about 500 rupees, which is 2,000 rupees a month. Add 600 rupee local cleaner, and a customer would otherwise spend 2,600 rupees. That's our ceiling. So I'd price it close to a thousand or twelve hundred rupees. That's well below the 2,600 a customer would otherwise spend, and close enough to the local cleaner to feel worth it, with slightly more premium. At 1,000 rupees, a customer in a scattered home loses us about 150 rupees every month, while one in a society makes about 250 rupees. So we start off with the launch in societies where at least 30 cars sign up. That gives us a 25% margin, and every new customer in the same building makes it better.
