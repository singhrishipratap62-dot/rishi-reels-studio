# Behind the Edit: "Claude can edit your videos only once you teach it your taste"
### A 33-second reel, four drafts, and one style I threw away

*by @rishi.docx*

This is the reel where I actually taught Claude my taste. The finished video took **four drafts**, a 28-point feedback round, and scrapping an entire editing style I'd spent a day building. Here's what happened.

---

## Draft 1: the style I threw away
I'd trained Claude on one creator's style: 15 of her reels broken down into a rule book, a look book and 18 named scene types. Detailed, measured, technically correct.

I edited this reel with it. Verdict: **"not as good as I aimed."** It looked generic, like someone else's video with my face in it.

**Lesson:** copying one creator closely doesn't give you *your* style. So instead of one style, Claude built an **options board** mixing ideas from five style systems I'd made, 5 options per moment, and I picked and mixed. That mix became my style.

## Drafts 2 → 4: the corrections
- Draft 2 → 3: **28 notes.** Almost all variations of one word: *smooth.* Pops, snaps, sudden entries all went. A half-rendered letter glitch. Cards now open from a thin line into a rectangle.
- Storyboard 3: "a circle has no personality" → an organic painted shape. My face pushed too low in the split → find the middle ground.
- Draft 3 → 4: the opening, caption height ("slightly", meaning about 30 px, not 120), one-word captions on busy screens, keep the comment sheet to the very end.

Every note went into a lessons file. That's why the next reel (the "EDIT" one) took one draft.

---

## The final edit, beat by beat

**0.0–0.7 s · opening**
The Claude logo (on a white disc) and a framed card of the video **slide out from behind my head**.
*Why:* the hook names a tool and a video; both appear in under a second, with depth.

**0.7–2.9 s · "Claude mein video daal ke ye prompt daal doge toh aisa kuch ban jaayega"**
My face **shrinks into a Claude chat** as an attached video. A prompt types, gets highlighted, is sent, and a result card opens showing my own clip.
*Why:* it puts the viewer inside the naive shortcut everyone believes in before I break it.

**2.9–5.3 s · "aisa kuch nahi hota hai. this doesn't work like that."**
**Black and white**, camera tracking my nose. No words on screen.
*Why:* a verdict belongs on the face. I first had "nope." on screen; I removed it, because the face says it better.

**5.3–8.4 s · "duniya bhar ki skills ikattha kar lo, GitHub repos ikattha kar lo"**
A **split pane** drops from the top; skill cards arrive one by one, then dark repo cards glide in. My face stays in the lower half.
*Why:* hoarding is about quantity, so make the quantity visible, and keep me on screen.

**8.5–11.5 s · "jab tak aap Claude ko apna taste ya design skill nahi samjha paoge…"**
A **design-skill.md** document beside a painted blue shape with words orbiting it: palette, rhythm, sound design, pacing, texture, timing, typography.
*Why:* "taste" is abstract. A real file plus the ingredients of taste makes it tangible.

**12.7–14.6 s · "ye poori video bhi end-to-end Claude se edited hai"**
An **edit timeline card behind my head**, with a slow zoom hiding a jump cut.
*Why:* proof about the video itself, while my face stays dominant.

**14.6–17.5 s · "why does this look better than all the Claude slop out there?"**
Zoom out: my face becomes **one tile in a wall of identical "slop" tiles**. Starts exactly on "why". One word of caption at a time, inside the tile below me.
*Why:* literally showing me as one of the crowd, then the next line explains why I'm not.

**18.5–27.5 s · "maine usko samjhaaya… kya karna hai, kaise karna hai… autonomously"**
**Picture-in-picture** under one card that stays while its contents swap: first the skill files ("what to do", "how to do it", progress to 100%), then a pipeline that ticks through to "autonomous". Back to full screen only after the sentence ends.
*Why:* steps need room, and keeping the same card makes it feel like one continuous process improving.

**29 s → end · "comment EDIT and I'll send you a step-by-step breakdown"**
Instagram comment sheet with a keyboard, "EDIT" typed, tap, green tick, held to the last frame.

## What made it feel premium
1. **Smooth over fast.** Long, soft movements (0.6–1.5 s). Speed comes from cutting on words, not quick animations.
2. **Face as the spine.** I'm on screen or inside every graphic.
3. **Every graphic says the spoken word.** "what to do" on screen when I say "kya karna hai".
4. **Real over fake.** Real logos, real file names, my real clip as the "result".
5. **Dense but quiet sound.** About 170 sound effects in 33 seconds, most of them barely audible.
6. **Captions:** serif hero word, small sans around it, romanised Hinglish exactly as spoken, adapting to each scene.

## What Claude got wrong (so you're ready)
Snappy animations, glitchy letters, a drawn gap around my head that I read as real, over-correcting small notes, a circle where I wanted something organic. Every one became a rule. That's the whole method.
