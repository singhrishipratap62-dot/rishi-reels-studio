# Behind the Edit: "Maine ye poori video Claude se edit ki hai"
### A 16-second reel, beat by beat

*by @rishi.docx*

This is the reel that sent you here. Here's every decision in it: what's on screen, why it's there, and what I changed along the way.

**What I gave Claude:** the raw clip and its subtitle file. **What I did:** picked from the options it drew for me and gave notes. **What Claude did:** the trim, the plan, every graphic, the captions, the sound, the checks.

---

## How it was made
1. **Auto-trim.** Claude cut 1.7 s of silence and unclear sound from the start and 1.5 s from the end. The reel became 15.9 s. (On the options board I'd marked both trim options "No, very basic, just do it." Now it just does it and tells me.)
2. **Options board.** For each moment (hook, proof, list, explanation, contrast, call to action) Claude drew 5 different options as phone mock-ups over real frames. I picked and mixed.
3. **Storyboard.** My picks frame by frame, with a box of assumptions it wasn't sure about ("you said top-left once and top-right once; I used top-right").
4. **Draft, notes, sound, check.**

## Beat by beat
*Times are approximate (seconds into the trimmed reel).*

**0.0–2.0 s · "okay, so this is going to be a very short reel."**
A small **timer pill** rises from *behind my head*, holds above it, then glides to the corner and counts down for the whole reel.
*Why:* the line literally says "short reel", so the first graphic makes that visible within half a second, and the countdown gives a reason to stay. Coming from behind my head adds depth without leaving my face.

**2.0–3.4 s · "maine ye poori video Claude se edit ki hai"**
An **editing timeline card** (lanes for video, captions, graphics, sound, with Claude's name) appears *behind my head*, with a slow zoom on me.
*Why:* this is the proof line. Showing an actual edit timeline says "Claude edited this" faster than any caption. The zoom also hides a jump cut.

**3.3–5.4 s · "mujhe ye cheez seekhne mein hafte ya mahine lag gaye"**
**Split screen:** my real **drafts 1 → 4** of another reel, plus three skill-file cards.
*Why:* "weeks or months" is a claim; real drafts are the evidence. I approved this as a reusable rule: *for effort beats, show the real work.*

**5.4–8.4 s · "end-to-end usse badhiya edit kaise karwa sakte ho"**
I shrink into a **picture-in-picture** under a pipeline card: Analyse · Plan · Build · Captions · Export. Each step ticks, and **the last tick lands on "badhiya edit"**.
*Why:* "end-to-end" is a process, so show the process. Timing the last tick to the payoff word makes it feel deliberate.

**8.4–10.3 s · "warna har doosri video toh slop lagti hai"**
**Black and white**, the camera gently tracking my nose. "slop" and "lagti hai." sit on screen from the start, while the line above shows one word at a time.
*Why:* a verdict lands on the face, not on a graphic. Draining colour makes "slop" feel dull without a single extra element. (The transcript said "slow". It was "slop". Always check words that change the meaning.)

**10.3–11.5 s · "agar aapko bhi ye cheez seekhni hai, samajhni hai"**
Same caption, **only the hero word changes**: *seekhni hai,* → *samajhni hai,*
*Why:* a repeated phrase with one new word reads as emphasis if only that word moves.

**11.5 s → end · "comment kar do EDIT…"**
My face slides up, blurred; an **Instagram comment sheet with a full keyboard** rises; "EDIT" types itself; a tap; the send button turns green with a tick; a **DM "resource" card** slides in. It stays until the last frame.
*Why:* show the exact action you want people to take. Holding it to the end means the last thing you see is the instruction.

## The style rules behind it
- Smooth, never snappy. Cards open from a thin line into a rectangle with a light blur, never "pop".
- My face is always on screen or inside the graphic (behind it, in the PiP, above the sheet).
- Captions: serif italic hero word + small sans support, romanised Hinglish exactly as I said it.
- Real things over mock-ups: real logos, real skill-file names, my real drafts, my own face as the avatar.
- Sound: dozens of quiet effects; a soft click on each key word, swooshes on every big move.

- 15.9 s, 7 beats, a graphic change roughly every 2 seconds.
