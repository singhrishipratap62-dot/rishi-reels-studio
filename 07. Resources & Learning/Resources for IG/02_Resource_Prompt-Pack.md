# The Claude Editing Prompt Pack
### 10 prompts for every stage of an AI-assisted edit

*by @rishi.docx*

These are the prompts I'd give a friend starting out. They follow the same order as my editing loop: **study → style card → plan → options → build → feedback → sound → check → remember.** Copy them, swap the [brackets], and keep them in one Claude Project so your files and notes stay together.

**Two rules that make every prompt work better:**
1. **One stage per message.** Never "plan and build this". Claude builds before you've agreed on anything.
2. **Always attach your Style Card and LESSONS file** (you'll make both below). They're how Claude remembers your taste.

---

## 1. Study your references
> I'm attaching contact sheets (frames in order) and transcripts from [N] reels in one editing style I love. Study them like a senior editor and tell me:
> - the caption system (closest Google Fonts, size, position, words at a time, how key words are emphasised, how words appear)
> - every scene/graphic type you see: give each a short name, what it looks like, when it's used
> - how things enter and leave the screen (speed, blur, slide, fade, pop)
> - pacing: how often the screen changes, when the first graphic appears, how long the face stays alone
> - which kind of line in the script triggers which kind of visual
> Mark guesses as guesses. Don't describe anything you can't actually see.

## 2. Write the Style Card
> Using your analysis and my notes below, write a one-page Style Card I can reuse for every edit. Sections: feel in one sentence · captions · colours (with what each is for) · scene menu (6–10 named scenes + when to use each) · motion rules · pacing targets · sound · never do. Keep it short and specific. Use numbers where you can.
> My notes on what I like: [paste your one line per reference]

*Then edit it yourself until it sounds like you. This is the most important file you'll own.*

## 3. Clean the transcript
> Here's the transcript of my video with timestamps. Fix obvious mishearings but don't rewrite what I said. List every word you changed or are unsure about so I can check it. [If Hinglish: write Hindi words in Roman script exactly as spoken, never translate.]

## 4. Plan the edit (then stop)
> Using my Style Card and LESSONS, make an edit plan. One row per line: time · exact words · what the line is doing (hook / claim / example / list / number / proof / contrast / call to action) · 3 visual options from my scene menu, one sentence each · your recommendation · why. Mark lines that should stay as just my face with captions. Add a pacing check (first graphic time, % of video with graphics, longest stretch of plain face). **Don't design or build anything yet. Wait for my picks.**

## 5. Get options for the big moments
> For [the hook / the key point / the call to action], give me 5 genuinely different options. For each: a name, one line describing it, how it moves, and how long it lasts. Make at least one option simpler than you'd normally suggest and one bolder.

*Pick, or mix: "B as the base, with A's colours and C's entrance." Claude handles mixes well.*

## 6. Build
> Build the edit from the approved plan: [paste picks]. Order: captions first, then each scene in timeline order. Follow the Style Card exactly; if something in the plan conflicts with it, ask before choosing. When done, list any assumptions you made.

*Path A (no code): ask for each scene as a design you can recreate or export into your editor. Path B (Claude Code with local tools): Claude builds and renders it directly.*

## 7. Turn feedback into fixes
> Here are my notes on this draft: [paste, timestamps help]. Turn them into a numbered fix list (timestamp · problem · what I expect · your fix). Fix everything in one pass and keep the previous draft untouched. If a note is ambiguous, show me 2–3 interpretations before building.

*Tip: if you say "a little", tell Claude what that means to you. Mine: about 30 pixels, not 120.*

## 8. Sound pass (only after the visuals are approved)
> Now add sound design. Rule: lots of sound effects, each one quiet. A soft click when a key caption word appears, a very quiet flick for other words, long smooth swooshes on big transitions (peak in the middle of the move), small pops when cards land. First 5 seconds slightly louder. Give me a cue sheet: time · sound · level · reason. You can't hear, so tell me what to check on my phone.

## 9. Final check before export
> Run a final check on this edit: captions inside Instagram's safe zone (nothing under the buttons on the right or the caption at the bottom), nothing white on white, every caption readable for at least half a second, no blank or black frames, export 1080×1920 at 30 fps, loudness around −14 to −16 LUFS. Tell me what you checked and what you couldn't check.

## 10. Remember what I taught you
> Add my notes from this edit to LESSONS as short rules, but only the ones about my general taste (not one-off fixes for this video). Newest at the bottom. Read LESSONS before every future edit; its rules outrank the Style Card.

---

### Bonus: the handoff prompt for long projects
> Read plan.md. Continue from the next unchecked step. At the end, tick what's done and add a two-line session note.

Start a new chat for each big stage with this. It saves a lot of usage and Claude never loses the thread.
