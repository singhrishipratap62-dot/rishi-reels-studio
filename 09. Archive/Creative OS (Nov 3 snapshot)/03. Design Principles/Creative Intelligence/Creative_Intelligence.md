# Creative Intelligence

**Core First Principle:** Creative output is a search process. Quality depends on (a) how wide you search, (b) how precisely you define what you're looking for, and (c) how fast you reject bad options. "Taste" is the speed and accuracy of rejection. Systemize the search so taste is spent only on the final choice.

## Strategic Frameworks

- **Define the job before the look.** Every visual decision answers one question: *what must the viewer feel, know, or do in this exact second?* If you can't name the job, the visual is decoration and should be cut.
- **Reverse-engineer from the outcome, not the reference.** Copying a viral edit copies its surface. Decompose instead:
  1. What retention problem did each choice solve?
  2. What mechanism solved it (curiosity gap, pattern break, payoff tease)?
  3. What is the minimal version of that mechanism in your context?
- **Constraint-driven lateral thinking.** When stuck, impose an arbitrary constraint, such as "no B-roll," "one shot only," "text only," or "tell it backwards." Constraints force the search out of the default patterns you've already tried.
- **Abstraction ladder for dead-ends.** If a visual idea fails, climb one level of abstraction:
  - Stuck: "Show the founder's growth."
  - Up: "Show change over time."
  - Up again: "Show contrast between two states."
  - Back down to new concrete options: split-screen, a match cut between old and new, the same location then vs. now, a counter ticking up.
- **Separate generation from judgment.** Generating and evaluating at the same time cuts the option space too early. Generate 5–10 options without filtering, then judge against the job definition.
- **Decision logs compound.** Record *why* each non-obvious choice worked or failed (retention graph dips, comments). This feeds the copilot: the knowledge base should hold reasons, not just presets.
- **Default to deletion.** The highest-leverage creative move is removal. Before adding an element, ask what could be removed so the remaining elements carry the meaning.

## Tactical Execution & Trade-offs

| Technique | Primary Use Case | Psychological Effect | The "Common Trap" vs. Pro Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hook deconstruction (frame-by-frame teardown)** | Studying competitors' high-retention reels | Builds a library of *mechanisms*, not looks | **Trap:** saving references as "vibes" and replicating fonts, transitions and music. **Fix:** log each hook as [visual trigger] + [cognitive gap] + [payoff timing]. Re-skin the mechanism with your own assets. |
| **Constraint injection** | Creative block, repetitive output, a stale series format | Novel output signals novelty to the audience and resets pattern fatigue | **Trap:** constraints so loose they still allow the default solution. **Fix:** pick constraints that make your current default *impossible* (e.g., "the hook cannot show a face"). |
| **First-principles teardown of a dead-end** | A scene that "doesn't work" and you can't say why | Turns vague dissatisfaction into a fixable, specific failure | **Trap:** layering effects, music or zooms to cover the problem. **Fix:** diagnose in order: (1) Is the information clear? (2) Is the emotion matched? (3) Is the timing right? (4) Only then, is the aesthetic right? Most dead-ends are failures of 1 or 3. |
| **Decision systemization (rule tables)** | Scaling a style across editors or an AI copilot | Consistent output builds a recognizable brand signature | **Trap:** rigid presets applied regardless of context, which turns into formula and fatigue. **Fix:** write rules as *condition → action → reason*. The reason lets an editor or the copilot break the rule intelligently. |

### Dead-End Diagnostic Order

| Order | Question | Typical fix |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Does the viewer know what they're looking at within 0.5s? | Simplify the frame; remove competing elements |
| 2 | Is the emotion of the visual matched to the line? | Swap the shot, change the music cue, change the scale |
| 3 | Is the timing right (too early, too late, too long)? | Slide the cut ±3–6 frames; trim the hold |
| 4 | Is the aesthetic serving or distracting? | Strip effects; return to clean footage |

## Retention & Funnel Application

- **Hook formula as a reusable system.** Catalogue hooks by mechanism: *contradiction, stakes, open loop, visual anomaly, outcome-first.* For each new reel, choose the mechanism first, then the execution. This prevents defaulting to the same hook type.
- **Use retention graphs as creative feedback.** Every dip is a dead-end to diagnose with the order above. Every spike is a mechanism to catalogue. Over time the knowledge base becomes data-backed taste.
- **Funnel-matched creativity.**
  - **Top of funnel:** novelty and pattern-breaks, maximizing exploration.
  - **Bottom of funnel:** clarity and proof, so minimize creative risk there. Novelty in a sales video costs trust.
- **Series formats = systemized creativity.** A fixed skeleton (same hook slot, same reveal structure) with a variable "creative slot" each episode gives the audience familiarity and novelty at the same time.
- **Copilot prompt rule:** when querying this knowledge base, state the *job* ("hold attention through a 4-second technical explanation") instead of the *look* ("make it dynamic"). Retrieval is only as good as the problem definition.
