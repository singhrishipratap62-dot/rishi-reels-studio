# Composition & Layout

**Core First Principle:** The eye moves to resolve imbalance. Every composition is a field of unresolved forces (weight, direction, open space), and the viewer keeps watching while there is tension left to resolve. Balanced frames are restful; tense frames hold attention. Editing manages how tension builds and releases from shot to shot.

## Strategic Frameworks

- **Tension is retention.** A perfectly centered, symmetrical frame resolves instantly, and the brain disengages. Use symmetry as a deliberate "full stop" (authority, finality) and asymmetry as the default "continue" state.
- **Grids are tools for motion, not static rules.** Thirds and phi grids matter less for where the subject sits than for where the subject moves *between* cuts. Alternating the subject between the left and right power points on cuts creates lateral eye movement that feels like momentum.
- **The golden ratio is a tension dial, not magic.** Phi (0.618) positions sit between thirds (0.667) and center (0.5). Use it when thirds feel too restless and center feels too static, for example in calm, explanatory segments. Don't believe the claim that phi is inherently "more beautiful"; it's simply a moderate tension setting.
- **Lead space reads as intent.** Space in front of the subject's gaze or motion means "going somewhere"; space behind means "something is coming." Give gaze direction lead space by default, and reverse it only to create unease.
- **Negative space is an attention vacuum.** Large empty regions don't hold the eye; they push it toward the nearest mass. Use negative space to *aim* attention, and reserve it as the slot for text so the composition doesn't need repainting when captions land.
- **The 9:16 frame works vertically.** Vertical frames scan top to bottom with less horizontal scan. In 9:16, place the face on the upper third (eyes ~30–38% from the top), keep the middle for gesture and props, and reserve the lower third for captions above the platform UI.
- **Edge contact creates tension.** Objects touching or nearly touching the frame edge, or each other, cause discomfort. Either give clear separation or a decisive overlap. A near-miss tangent looks like an error.

## Tactical Execution & Trade-offs

| Technique | Primary Use Case | Psychological Effect | The "Common Trap" vs. Pro Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Centered symmetry** | Direct-to-camera authority statements, the thesis line, CTAs | Confrontation, certainty, "this is the point" | **Trap:** using it for the whole talking head, which makes the frame feel static and drop-off rises. **Fix:** reserve centered framing for payoff moments. Cut into it so the shift itself signals importance. |
| **Asymmetric thirds / phi placement** | Default talking head, interviews, B-roll | Ongoing low-level tension; implies conversation and movement | **Trap:** subject on a third with dead space on the other side. **Fix:** fill the open side with purposeful content (graphic, prop, depth, light source) or use it as the text slot. |
| **Punch-in / reframe cuts (scale grid)** | Jump-cut talking heads, emphasizing key lines | Each reframe resets attention; the scale jump reads as emphasis | **Trap:** random punch-ins every cut, which become noise and stop signaling anything. **Fix:** use 2–3 fixed scale tiers (wide 100%, medium ~115–125%, tight ~140–160%) and map them to emphasis levels. Tight is reserved for the payload line. |
| **Negative-space framing** | Lines heavy with text, quote moments, emotional beats | Isolation, weight, gravity; one idea at a time | **Trap:** empty space with nothing claiming it, which reads as a framing error. **Fix:** compose the shot with the text block already planned; the space should look "reserved," not leftover. |

### Grid Selection by Intent

| Intent | Grid / Position | Tension level |
|---|---|---|
| Authority, finality, CTA | Center, symmetrical | Low (resolution) |
| Calm explanation, long-form teaching | Phi (0.382 / 0.618) | Medium-low |
| Energy, conversation, default reels | Thirds (0.333 / 0.667) | Medium-high |
| Unease, disruption, pattern-break | Extreme edge placement, tilted horizon, reversed lead space | High |

## Retention & Funnel Application

- **Hook frame = maximum tension.** Open on an off-balance frame: an extreme close-up, the subject mid-motion, or an object entering the frame. The viewer's need to resolve the frame carries them past the first 1–2 seconds.
- **Alternate placement to create motion without movement.** On static talking heads, alternate between left-third and right-third crops on alternate cuts, or alternate scale tiers. Viewers perceive pace even though nothing moves.
- **Compose for overlays in advance.** Shooting and framing with fixed text slots (upper-right or lower third above the UI) keeps graphics from covering the face. A face covered by text costs more attention than the text earns.
- **Long-form: use composition to mark sections.** Change the base framing per chapter (e.g., medium for the setup, tighter for the core argument). Structural changes feel like progress and counter the "this is going on forever" sense.
- **CTA composition rule:** subject centered, direct eye line, negative space on the side where the CTA graphic lands. Symmetry signals "we're at the conclusion," and the shift from asymmetric to symmetric framing works as an unconscious closing cadence.
