# Pacing and structure

How a rishi.docx reel is shaped in time. Distilled from the motion references.

## The shape: face → graphics → face

| Part | Time | What's on screen |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0–2s | `SplitHook`: the strongest visual on top, you below, bold title. Payoff or surprising image shown immediately. |
| Release | ~2s | Split resolves to full-screen visual (hard cut or flash cut). |
| Context | 2–8s | Graphics/footage that ground the hook. You can return briefly (1–2s) to say the key line on camera. |
| Body | middle | Chapters. Each chapter: `ChapterDial` (≈1s) → visuals building with the voice. You appear at chapter pivots, 1–3s each. |
| Takeaway | last 5–8s | You on camera, full frame, saying the usable line. |
| CTA | last 2–4s | You on camera, or `FollowCard` / CTA card. |

Talking head ≈ 10–25% of runtime. The rest is visuals.

## Beat rules

- **A new visual element every 1–2 seconds.** If a graphic has been static for 3s, something must arrive, move in, or the shot must cut.
- **One beat = one spoken idea.** A beat starts on the word that introduces it.
- **Cut hard between scenes.** Don't animate transitions between unrelated visuals; cut on a word.
- **Build within a scene.** Inside one graphic, add elements in place rather than cutting.
- **Return to the face to change gear**: a new chapter, a strong opinion, or a direct address ("and that's the real problem").

## The split hook

- Top 60% (y 0–1150): the most striking visual of the topic — result, product, footage, number.
- Bottom 40% (y 1150–1920): you, framed from chest up, looking at camera.
- Title over the bottom edge of the top half: 2–5 words, `display` uppercase or sentence case, words arriving one by one (≈100ms apart).
- Hold 1.2–2s, then cut to full-screen.

## Footage and real media

- Real sources (articles, posts, apps, papers, clips) always appear inside a frame: `WindowFrame`, `PostCard`, `MediaFrame`, or a phone.
- Landscape footage in a vertical reel: centre it full-width, letterboxed, with the caption below it.
- Screen recordings: crop tight to the action, zoom in (1.2–1.6×) on what matters, keep the cursor visible.
- B-roll (hands typing, devices, places) can cut in for 1–2s between graphics to rest the eye.

## Two shapes of reel

Reference set 2 showed a second, equally strong shape. Pick one per reel.

| | **Graphics-led** (default above) | **Face-led explainer** |
|---|---|---|
| Talking head | 10–25% of runtime | 40–60% of runtime |
| Best for | AI news decoded, tools, launches with lots of real material | AI stories, opinion, anything where you are the reason to watch |
| Cutaways | Long graphic sequences, 5–20s | Short explainer plates, 2–4s each, one idea each |
| Keeping the face lively | — | Punch-in jump cuts (wide ↔ tight) every 2–3 sentences; b-roll inserts of 1–2s |
| Opening | `SplitHook` | `TitleOverFace` (title over the top of the frame for 2–4s), or `SplitHook` |

In a face-led reel, cut away whenever a sentence has a picture in it (a number, a place, a person, an object, a document), and come back to the face for the reaction or the "so what".

## Explainer plates

The cutaway unit of a face-led reel: one plate, one idea, held 2–4s.

1. **Ground**: paper (default) or ink. Crop-mark corners only rarely (see *Taste rules*).
2. **Kicker**: tiny spaced uppercase line between hairlines (`— THE PROBLEM —`, `— UNTIL 2020 —`).
3. **Title**: `ConceptTitle` (serif) or `HookTitle` (sans), 1–2 lines.
4. **One visual**: a halftone object, `UnitDots`, `DotMap`, `PortraitCard`, a UI card, a `DataTable`.
5. **Names**: `TabLabel`s on anything that needs naming.
6. **Captions** continue over it.

Build it in 2–3 steps on the voice (title → visual → labels), never all at once.

## Captions in the timeline

`Captions` run through the whole reel, including over graphics. When someone other than you is speaking (interview clip, quote), their captions switch to the quoted-speaker style.
