# Editing psychology: Rishi v2

> Why scenes are chosen the way they are. Defaults, not rules.

## 1. Core ideas
1. **The face is the spine.** Open, verdict, payoff and CTA happen on the face. Graphics either sit around the face or carry the face inside them (attachment, tile, PiP, blurred above the sheet), so the viewer never loses the person. `[RES][R]`
2. **Announce on the face, explain with a picture.** A new claim or reaction → face. An explanation, a list or proof → a graphic that shows it literally. `[ME]`
3. **Show his real world.** Real tools (Claude, GitHub, Instagram UI), his real skill files, his own clip as the "result", his own face sticker. Stand-ins are a last resort. `[R]`
4. **Premium = calm + smooth.** One idea per screen, few elements, white/ice grounds, slow soft motion. Speed comes from cutting on the words, not from fast animation. `[R]`
5. **Every graphic says the spoken word.** Card text, labels and ticks mirror the line ("what to do", "how to do it", "autonomously"). `[ME][DP]`

## 2. Beat → scene (run per line)
| The line is… | Default | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Hook naming a tool + promise | S1 + S2 | puts the viewer inside the naive shortcut |
| Reaction / negation | S3 B&W track (no words) | the verdict lands on his face |
| A list of things people collect | S4 split pane | quantity is visible; the face stays |
| A thesis about taste/skill | S5 doc + figure | "taste" made tangible: a real skill file + an abstract figure |
| Proof about the video itself | S6 behind-head card | depth plus the meta claim; the face stays dominant |
| "Unlike everyone else's…" | S7 wall zoom-out | he literally becomes one tile among identical ones |
| How-to / steps | S8 PiP + card | the steps get room; the face stays present |
| Iteration → autonomy | S8 contents swap | continuity: same card, the work moves on |
| CTA comment keyword | S9 comments sheet | shows the exact action to take |
| Opinion, bridge, joke | A-roll + captions | not everything needs a graphic |

## 3. Pacing and retention
- First graphic within ~0.7 s; the hook is the densest part. `[ME][S]`
- Graphics ≈45–55 % of the runtime; never more than ~8 s of plain A-roll in the body. `[ME]`
- Cut **on the trigger word** (zoom-outs start exactly on "why"). Return to the face only **after** the line finishes ("after intervention"). `[R]`
- No empty white beats; overlap exits and entrances. `[R]`
- Hide jump cuts inside motion (cut-hiding zoom, graphic transitions). `[R]`
- Hold finished states long enough to read (≥0.5 s); slower is fine if it's smooth. `[R][ME]`
- Keep the CTA sheet up until the very end. Ending on the CTA beats ending on a bare face for this format. `[R]`

## 4. Captions as psychology
- Hero words carry the meaning in serif; the support words fade up as they're said. Viewers read in sync with the voice.
- Single-word captions when the screen is busy (wall, CTA), so the eye has one thing to read.

## 5. Anti-patterns
- Decorative graphics with no link to the words.
- Busy screens (more than 2 moving focal points), pops and snaps, fast springs.
- Cutting away from the face for the verdict line.
- Generic AI-looking mock-ups (purple gradients, rocket emoji) used as anything but the "slop" being mocked.
